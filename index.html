04 · Will it spill?

Two numbers decide it: what’s in McPhee on April 1, and what spring brings.

Since 1988, McPhee has spilled in 21 of 39 springs, though only 4 of the last 15. Nearly every one of those springs follows the same rule: once April 1 storage plus April–July inflow passes about 486,000 acre-feet, the lake fills and roughly nine of every ten acre-feet beyond that go down the river.

05 · Out to the fields

Most of the Dolores leaves through the side door, into the San Juan basin.

McPhee’s water serves roughly 63,000 irrigated acres: full service around Dove Creek and on Ute Mountain Ute land near Towaoc, and supplemental water for Montezuma Valley Irrigation Company farms. Full-service farms get 22 inches an acre in a full-supply year. In 2026, DWCD called the supply “abysmal.” Canals run from spring to fall; in winter the tiles above read off.

At Bedrock

Outside of spills, the river below the dam runs on a fish pool, roughly 30,000 acre-feet in a full-supply year and far less in dry ones, released on a schedule set with Colorado Parks and Wildlife. Boatable water needs a spill, or a storm on the tributaries like this one.
